# YouTube banger titles

Source: Richard the YouTube strategist (@Richard_YTS), "100 Viral Title Formats to Blow Up Your YouTube Channel in 2026" (drawn from 10,000+ outlier videos).

## How to write the title
1. Name the single most surprising true fact or visual in the video.
2. Pick 2-3 formats below that fit it.
   - For news, tech or AI clips, favour: 1, 7, 18, 27, 30, 40, 53, 57, 58, 66, 67, 68, 73, 75, 79, 84, 92, 94.
   - For money, investing or crypto, favour: 6, 21, 22, 26, 42, 43, 63, 74, 81, 89.
3. Fill in the format with concrete specifics (place, year, number, name). Keep it under about 60 characters and use title case. Use ALL CAPS on one word at most.
4. Open a curiosity gap but never lie. Every claim must be backed by the video or its source. Never use fake numbers.
5. For Shorts, append #Shorts. Give 3 options, mark your pick, and note which format each one uses.

## The 100 formats (with why each works)
1. Something Weird Is Happening In [Place / Group] — It highlights an unexplained change in a place/group the viewer already knows without saying what it is, so the only way to close the curiosity gap is to click.
2. It's [Painful Adjective], But It'll [Desired Outcome] — It admits up front that the method is boring or brutal, which makes the promise feel more realistic or "legit.".
3. Give Me [X Minutes] And I'll Make You [Dangerously / Disgustingly] Good At [Skill] — It trades a small, specific time commitment for a huge transformation, so the click feels low-risk.
4. I Gave Myself [30 Days] To [Ambitious Goal] — A hard deadline plus a specific goal turns a tutorial into a challenge with stakes, and viewers stay to find out whether it worked.
5. I Tried [Thing] For [Time Period] (This Is What Happened) — It promises a first-hand result the viewer doesn't have to risk getting themselves.
6. [Number] Things That Are No Longer Worth Your Money — It tells viewers they might be wasting money right now, which feels urgent and personal.
7. The New Era Of [Topic] Has Just Begun — It announces a big shift in a space the viewer cares about and implies the old rules don't work anymore.
8. I'm [Age]. If You're In Your [Age]s, Watch This — It speaks directly to one age group with the authority of someone a few years ahead of them, so it feels like advice from an older sibling or mentor.
9. Unfortunately, [Difficult Goal] Really Is This Simple — "Unfortunately" is a twist: it suggests the answer is so simple that viewers have no excuse left.
10. [Skill] Is Hard Until You [Learn This] — It validates the viewer's frustration and then promises a simple lesson or trick that makes it easy.
11. How To [Train / Trick] Your Brain To [Do Hard Things] — It reframes discipline as a brain hack, which feels more achievable than relying on willpower.
12. This [Video / Message] Will Find You Right Before [Something Good Happens] — It makes the viewer feel the video arrived for them at exactly the right moment, which is hard to scroll past.
13. The Rise And Fall Of [Person / Brand] — It promises a full story arc with a dramatic ending, which works for almost any subject with a public peak and collapse.
14. I Wasted [Time] [Doing Thing] (Until I Learned This) — The creator admits a painful mistake, which makes them relatable and credible at the same time.
15. Don't Buy [Product] Until You Watch This — It catches people at the exact moment before a purchase, when they are most afraid of making a mistake.
16. Ranking Every [Category] From Worst To Best — A complete ranking promises a definitive verdict, and viewers click to see where their favorite lands.
17. The Psychology Of People Who [Behavior] — It turns a familiar behavior into a personality insight, so viewers click to learn something about themselves or someone they know.
18. The Dark Side Of [Topic] — It promises the hidden downside of something that looks appealing or successful from the outside.
19. Stop [Common Habit] (Do This Instead) — It tells viewers that something they are already doing is wrong, which triggers instant self-doubt.
20. How I Would [Achieve Goal] (If I Had To Start Over) — An expert showing you how they would start from scratch is exactly what a beginner wants.
21. How [Business / Company] Actually Makes Money — Everyone uses these businesses but almost nobody knows the real profit engine behind them, so the title promises a hidden mechanism.
22. This [Store / Product] Makes $[Revenue]/[Period]... Here's How — A big, specific revenue number next to a surprisingly ordinary product produces curiosity (how did THIS product make THAT much?).
23. I [Survived / Spent] 24 Hours [In Place / With Person] — The 24-hour limit turns any setting into a contained challenge with a clear beginning and end.
24. I Ate [The Cheapest / Most Expensive / Only X] For 24 Hours — Food challenges with an extreme constraint are instantly understandable and visually satisfying.
25. I Studied [Number] [Type Of People]. Here's What They All Do — It compresses a huge amount of research into one pattern the viewer can copy.
26. Why [Thing] Got So Expensive — It names a price increase the viewer has personally felt and promises the hidden reason behind it.
27. The [Thing] Nobody Talks About — It positions the video as insider knowledge that the rest of the niche has overlooked.
28. What [Extreme Experience] Actually Feels Like — It answers a morbid question people wonder about but can never safely find out first-hand.
29. The Death Of [Thing] — Declaring something is dead is a bold claim that fans of that thing feel they need to respond to.
30. [Tool / Update] Just Changed Everything — It frames a new release as a turning point that anyone in the space needs to know about immediately.
31. Every [Category] Explained In [X] Minutes — "Every" promises completeness and the minute count promises speed, so viewers get a whole topic in one sitting.
32. [X] Years Of [Skill] Knowledge In [Y] Minutes — The gap between years of experience and a few minutes of watching makes the video feel like an instant shortcut.
33. I Was Wrong About [Thing] — A public admission of being wrong is rare and disarming, so it instantly builds trust.
34. I [Bought / Learned / Tried] [Thing] So You Don't Have To — The creator takes the risk, cost, or boredom on the viewer's behalf, so watching feels like getting a free verdict.
35. The [Real] Problem With [Thing] — It promises a sharp critique of something the audience loves, which fans can't resist checking.
36. How To [Be So Productive / Change Your Body So Fast] It Feels Illegal — "Feels illegal" exaggerates the result so much that it implies an unfair advantage.
37. [Number] [Tricks / Tools] That Feel Illegal To Know — It turns a simple list into forbidden knowledge, which makes even ordinary tips feel exclusive.
38. I Built [Impressive Thing] In [Short Time] — The curiosity created by an ambitious result achieved in a tiny time frame is what forces people to click.
39. The Most [Extreme Adjective] [Things] In [Domain] History — A superlative plus "history" promises the single most extreme examples ever recorded, so viewers expect a high energy/high stakes highlight reel.
40. What [Really] Happened To [Person / Brand]? — It targets people or things that were once everywhere and then disappeared, which triggers nostalgia and curiosity.
41. [Number] Things I Wish I Knew Before [Milestone] — Hindsight from someone further along feels like a cheat sheet, and the milestone tells the audience whether it's for them or not.
42. Why Rich People [Surprising Behavior] — Everyone loves knowing why rich people do what they do.
43. The #1 [Topic] Mistake [Most People] Make — Narrowing everything down to a single mistake makes the fix feel quick and high-impact.
44. I Quit My [Job] [To Do Thing]. Here's What Happened — Quitting a job is the dream many viewers fantasize about, so seeing the real outcome is nearly irresistible.
45. How To ACTUALLY [Achieve Goal] — "Actually" implies that all the other advice on this topic is fluff, and this video finally gives the real method.
46. 1 vs [Huge Amount] [Thing] — The extreme price/amount is easy to understand and promises a satisfying comparison.
47. I Am (Politely) Begging You To [Action] Before It's Too Late — The polite-but-desperate tone is a pattern interrupt while also making the video feel urgent.
48. [Bold Personal Statement]. Here's Why. — A short, surprising statement creates tension and "here's why" promises the resolution.
49. If You [Love Thing / Needed This Today], Here's Why — It calls out a specific trait or feeling in the viewer and promises to explain it.
50. I Tested Every [Category] — Testing everything in a category is more than any viewer would ever do themselves, so the video becomes the definitive verdict.
51. This Is Why You [Still] [Can't / Don't] [Skill] — It names a frustration the viewer has had for a while and promises the root cause in one video.
52. If I [Started / Wanted To] [Goal] In [Year], I'd Do This — It's a personal, current-year plan from someone experienced, which feels more actionable than generic advice.
53. Inside [Place's Most] [Superlative] [Place/ Thing] — "Inside" promises access to somewhere the viewer might not be able to go themselves, and the superlative tells them it's the most extreme example.
54. The World's Most [Forbidden / Dangerous] Places — Forbidden places trigger instant curiosity because the viewer is told they're not allowed to see them.
55. Watch This If You [Feel Stuck / Are In A Slump] — It targets a specific emotional state, so viewers who feel that way right now feel personally addressed.
56. The [Boring] Secret [Behind / That] [Desired Result] — "Secret" promises a hidden lever, and attaching it to a result the viewer wants (closer relationships, better drawings) makes it feel worth learning.
57. How [Company / Person] Changed [Thing] - The Untold Story — It pairs a massive, familiar name with a promise of hidden backstory.
58. The Untold Story Of [Subject] — Similar to the title above, "Untold" implies the viewer has only heard half of the story, even about something familiar.
59. I Ate [Nothing But / Only] [Food Or Restaurant] For [Time Period] — A strict and kind of ridiculous food rule makes for an entertaining and often funny challenge.
60. Why You Should Never [Action] — "Never" is a power word that instantly ups the stakes and warns the viewer away from something they may be doing or considering.
61. The Ultimate Beginner's Guide To [Topic] — It promises one complete resource for someone starting from zero, which saves them from piecing together random tutorials.
62. [Number] Of The Most [Adjective] Books [For Outcome] — A curated reading list from a trusted reader saves the viewer hours of searching.
63. Is [Thing] Still Worth It In [Year]? — It targets people on the fence about something that used to be a clear yes.
64. [99% / 0.1%] Of People [Know / Don't Know] These [Number] [Tricks] — The statistic implies the viewer is about to join a tiny, informed minority.
65. I Analyzed [Big Number] [Things]. Here's What I Found — A huge sample size signals serious research, and the viewer gets the conclusions without doing any of the work.
66. [Place] Is Hiding Something Nobody Talks About — It casts a familiar place as having a secret, which turns a geography video into a mystery.
67. They're Lying To You About [Thing] — It creates an "us vs them" story where the viewer has been deceived, which triggers both anger and curiosity.
68. The REAL Reason [Something Happens] — "Real" implies the common explanation is wrong or incomplete, so even people who think they know the answer click.
69. What They Don't Tell You About [Experience] — It promises the hidden downside or insider truth of something people romanticize or are curious about.
70. The End Of [Thing] — Declaring the end of something familiar sparks immediate concern from anyone who uses or loves it.
71. I [Bought / Visited] The World's Cheapest [Thing] — "Cheapest" sets low expectations and invites the viewer to see just how bad (or surprisingly good) it is.
72. [Option A] vs [Option B]: Which Is Better [In Year]? — Head-to-head comparisons settle a debate the audience is already having, so both camps click to see who wins.
73. Why Everyone [Is Suddenly / Quietly] [Doing Thing] — It points at a trend the viewer may have half-noticed and promises the explanation.
74. The Hidden Cost Of [Thing] — It tells viewers that something they see as free, cheap or harmless actually carries a cost they haven't noticed.
75. This Will Change How You See [Subject] [Forever] — It promises a perspective shift rather than just information, which feels more valuable.
76. [Topic]: Everything You Need To Know — It promises a complete, one-stop briefing on something new or confusing, so viewers don't have to piece it together themselves.
77. Why I Stopped [Doing Thing] — Quitting something is a dramatic personal decision, and viewers who do the same thing want to know if they should stop too.
78. The 7 Levels Of [Category] — This format is still ripping, and for a simple reason: A level system turns a topic into a progression the viewer can place themselves on, or promises the full arc from worst to best, cheapest to most expensive, etc.
79. How [Everyday Thing] Actually Works — It takes something people use constantly but never think about and reveals the hidden system behind it.
80. [Product] - [Time] Later: An Honest Review — Long-term reviews answer the question launch-day reviews can't: is it still good after the hype? "Honest" signals no sponsorship so it's more reliable.
81. I Paid $[Amount] For [Thing]. Here's What Happened — A specific price makes the stakes real, and viewers want to know whether the money was well spent or wasted.
82. The Easiest Way To [Achieve Goal] — It promises the lowest-effort path to a result people want, which appeals to beginners and busy viewers alike.
83. I Hired [Expert / Pro] To [Unexpected Task] — Bringing in a pro creates an instant mismatch and a fun payoff.
84. Exposing [Every Secret In / The Truth About] [Thing] — "Exposing" promises hidden information is about to come out, which feels exclusive and slightly dramatic.
85. [Product]: Hidden Features [You're Not Using] — It tells owners they're only using part of something they already paid for.
86. The Most Underrated [Thing] In [Domain] — It promises a hidden gem that most people overlook, which makes the viewer feel ahead of the crowd.
87. [Topic] That Make(s) No Sense — It highlights the absurd or contradictory side of something familiar, which triggers curiosity.
88. It Took Me [Years] To Learn What I'll Teach You In [Minutes] — The contrast between years of struggle and a few minutes of watching makes the video feel like a massive shortcut.
89. You're Wasting [X%] Of Your [Thing] — A specific percentage of waste makes the loss feel real and measurable.
90. I Tested Viral [Thing] — Viral content already has proven interest, and testing it answers the question everyone has: does it actually work? It's a fact-check with entertainment value.
91. You Need To Stop [Doing Thing] — A direct command creates urgency and tells the viewer they're making a mistake right now.
92. The Biggest [Lie / Scam] In [Domain] History — It promises the single most dramatic deception in a field, which combines outrage with a big story.
93. The ENTIRE History Of [Place] In [X] Minutes — It compresses centuries into an easily digestible video, which feels like a satisfying watch and a shortcut to extensive knowledge.
94. What Nobody Tells You About [Experience] — It promises the honest, behind-the-scenes reality of a life stage or goal.
95. Who Is The Best [Role] In [Group]? — Ranking the best in a group sparks debate and lets fans root for their favorite.
96. The Complete Guide To [Topic] — It promises everything the viewer needs in one place, so they don't have to piece together scattered tutorials.
97. Everything Wrong With [Thing] — A full critique of something popular appeals to both fans and haters.
98. Everything You Missed In [Movie / Show] — It makes fans feel they didn't fully see something they already watched, so they come back for a second layer of detail.
99. THIS Is How You [Do Something Right / Waste Talent] — It presents one example as the definitive model (or cautionary tale), which makes it feel like a masterclass.
100. You're Not [Lazy / Behind]. You're [Real Reason] — It removes guilt from the viewer and replaces it with a kinder, more accurate explanation.
